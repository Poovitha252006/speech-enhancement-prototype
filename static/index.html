ECE Engineering Project Report: Mathematical Theory & System Architecture

ECE Capstone & Prototype Submission | Speech Signal Processing

1. Problem Statement & Motivation

Real-world acoustic environments introduce complex interference consisting of three distinct mathematical noise classes:

  • Impulsive Noise (sharp spikes, clicks, pops, switch bursts) violating Gaussian linearity assumptions.
  • Stationary Noise (fan hum, AC rumble, 50/60 Hz electrical hum, white thermal hiss) possessing constant Power Spectral Density (PSD).
  • Non-Stationary Noise (human speech babble, cafe chatter, traffic roar, sirens) exhibiting rapid spectral flux that overlaps with target speech frequencies.

Traditional single-algorithm systems (such as simple spectral subtraction) suffer from severe "musical noise", vocal muffling, or complete failure against impulsive and non-stationary interferences. This prototype implements a Cascaded Hybrid Signal Processing Pipeline that decouples each noise class into dedicated processing stages.

3. Mathematical Formulations

A. Stage 1: Impulsive Outlier Detection (Hampel Filter)

For audio sequence \( x[n] \), compute the rolling median \( \mu_{1/2}[n] \) over window \( 2K+1 \):

\[ \text{MAD}[n] = 1.4826 \times \text{median}\left(|x[n+k] - \mu_{1/2}[n]|\right), \quad k \in [-K, K] \]

An impulse is flagged at index \( n \) when:

\[ |x[n] - \mu_{1/2}[n]| > \tau \cdot \text{MAD}[n] \quad \text{AND} \quad \left|\frac{dx}{dt}[n]\right| > \theta \]

Corrupted clusters \( [n_a, n_b] \) are reconstructed via Cubic Hermite Splines anchored on clean boundary points.

B. Stage 2: Decision-Directed Wiener Filtering (Ephraim-Malah)

In STFT domain with frequency bin \( k \) and frame \( m \):

\[ \gamma(k, m) = \frac{|Y(k, m)|^2}{\lambda_d(k, m)} \quad \text{(A Posteriori SNR)} \]

The a priori SNR \( \xi(k, m) \) is estimated recursively via the Decision-Directed approach:

\[ \xi(k, m) = \alpha \frac{|\hat{S}(k, m-1)|^2}{\lambda_d(k, m-1)} + (1-\alpha) \max\left(\gamma(k, m) - 1, 0\right) \]

The optimal Wiener Gain with spectral floor \( \beta_{\text{floor}} \) is:

\[ G(k, m) = \max\left(\frac{\xi(k, m)}{1 + \xi(k, m)}, \beta_{\text{floor}}\right) \]

C. Stage 3: Harmonic Comb & Bark Subband Masking

For voiced frames with fundamental pitch \( F_0 \), target speech exhibits energy peaks at integer harmonics \( h \cdot F_0 \). The harmonic comb transfer function is:

\[ H_{\text{harmonic}}(f) = \max_{h \in [1, H]} \left( \exp\left(-\frac{(f - h \cdot F_0)^2}{2 \sigma_h^2}\right) \right) \]

Inter-harmonic valleys where background babble resides are attenuated by up to 24 dB, isolating target speech.

Viva Voce Defense & Project Examination Guide

Key technical questions, justifications, and theoretical defense points for academic review and project evaluation.

Q1: Why is a single filter (e.g. Wiener or Spectral Subtraction) insufficient for all noise types? +

Answer: Linear frequency-domain filters make Gaussian stationarity assumptions. Impulsive noises (clicks/pops) have wideband infinite-harmonic characteristics in frequency domain; applying Wiener filtering smears the impulse in time, creating audible thuds or ringing. Conversely, non-stationary noises (babble) modulate faster than the noise PSD update rate of stationary filters. Decoupling the problem into time-domain outlier elimination (Stage 1), minimum statistics PSD tracking (Stage 2), and harmonic comb pitch tracking (Stage 3) ensures each noise type is suppressed with zero mutual interference.

Q2: What causes "musical noise" and how does this prototype eliminate it? +

Answer: Musical noise consists of isolated, randomly distributed spectral peaks in the residual noise floor caused by high variance in short-time spectral estimation. Our system eliminates musical noise through three mechanisms:
1) Ephraim-Malah Decision-Directed (DD) approach with smoothing factor \(\alpha = 0.96\), which smooths gain transitions across consecutive time frames.
2) Strict Spectral Floor (\(\beta \approx -30\text{ dB}\)) that prevents sudden drops to zero energy in adjacent frequency bins.
3) Multi-Band Over-subtraction that accounts for human psychoacoustic masking thresholds.

Q3: How does the system distinguish target speech from background speech babble? +

Answer: Human voiced vowels are characterized by a strong fundamental pitch frequency \( F_0 \) and phase-coherent integer harmonic overtones. Background babble is composed of uncorrelated multi-speaker chatter with conflicting, rapidly drifting pitch tracks. By estimating the primary speaker's \( F_0 \) via normalized autocorrelation and applying a Pitch-Synchronous Harmonic Comb Filter, our algorithm selectively amplifies the primary speaker's harmonic peaks while notching out the inter-harmonic valleys containing the diffuse babble energy.

Q4: What objective metrics are used to validate performance? +

Answer: The prototype evaluates:
• Global SNR & \(\Delta\)SNR (dB): Net signal power to noise power ratio gain.
• Segmental SNR (SSNR in dB): Averaged over 25ms active speech frames to avoid silence bias.
• Noise Reduction Factor (NRF in dB): Quantitative attenuation of ambient background power.
• Estimated PESQ (ITU-T P.862 / MOS-LQO 1.0 - 5.0): Psychoacoustic Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality.
• STOI (0.0 - 1.0): Short-Time Objective Intelligibility correlation measuring speech intelligibility.

Q5: What is the computational complexity and real-time feasibility? +

Answer: The pipeline operates with \( O(N \log N) \) FFT complexity per frame (frame length = 1024, hop size = 256 samples, which is 16 milliseconds latency at 16 kHz sampling rate). The algorithm runs in under 0.15× real-time on standard CPUs without requiring expensive GPU hardware, making it immediately deployable on edge devices, embedded DSPs, or telecommunication gateways.
